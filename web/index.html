Still scrubbing through lectures to find one slide?

Retrieve makes your lecture slides, PDFs and recording captions searchable on your phone. Type what you remember and get the module, the week, the slide, and the exact minute in the recording. Or ask a question and get a short answer that cites its sources.

From a folder of slides to the right minute.

Add a week of material once. After that, every question takes a few seconds.

Step 1

Import

Pick slides, PDFs and caption files and file them under a module and week.

Step 2

Index

Retrieve reads every page on the phone, including slides that are only pictures of text. A notification shows its progress.

Step 3

Search

Type what you remember. Narrow it with module: or week:4..6 when you know roughly where it was.

Step 4

Jump to it

Open the slide with your words highlighted, or the transcript at the moment it was said.

Built for the way you actually revise.

Your course is already organised by module and week. Retrieve keeps that structure, so every folder is also a search.

Search by meaning

Ask for “agreement between servers” and it finds the slide titled consensus. Keyword and meaning matches are combined.

A query language when you need it

Filter by module and week, search exact phrases, leave things out.

module:COMP26120 week:4..6 "amortised" NOT tutorial

Recordings, to the minute

Import the caption file from the lecture portal. Results open the transcript at the right timestamp.

See the slide itself

Results show the real page, with your search words highlighted. Swipe to the slides either side.

Organised like your course

Modules, then weeks, then documents, each with its own colour. Open a week and you’re already searching inside it.

Stays on your phone

No account, no cloud and no internet permission, so the app can’t send anything anywhere. Everything works in airplane mode.

Ask your lectures. Every answer shows where it came from.

Ask a question in plain words. Retrieve finds the passages that cover it, shows their key sentences word for word, and adds a short answer written on your phone, with a numbered link to every slide and recording moment it used.

  • Answers only from your material

    The model only sees passages from your own slides and captions, not the internet.

  • Every citation is checked

    Tap a number to open that slide, or the transcript at that minute. A citation that doesn’t match a passage the model was given is flagged.

  • It says when it doesn’t know

    If your material doesn’t cover the question, you get “Not in your materials” and an offer to search instead.

Questions students ask first

Does anything get uploaded?

No. Your files are read, indexed and searched on the phone. Retrieve has no account, no server and no internet permission, so Android won’t let it connect to anything. The privacy policy has the details.

Which files can I add?

PDF slides and documents, and WebVTT caption files (.vtt) from your lecture recording portal. Slides that are only images are read with on-device text recognition.

Can it search lecture recordings?

Yes, through their caption files. Each 30 seconds of captions becomes a search result, so you land on the right minute. It doesn’t transcribe audio itself.

What do I type?

Whatever you remember, in your own words. If you know roughly where it was, add filters like module:COMP26120 or week:4..6. The search tips in the app use your own module codes as examples.

Can Ask make things up?

The short answer can, like any language model, so Ask puts the key sentences from your slides first, copied word for word, and links every sentence to its source. It only sees passages from your own material, citations that don’t match those passages are flagged, and when nothing relevant is found it says “Not in your materials” instead of guessing.

Which phones does it run on?

Android 10 or later. Search is quick even on a 3 GB tablet. Ask runs a language model (Gemma 3 1B) on the phone: it works on that tablet too, but takes about 20 seconds to start writing, so a phone with more memory and a newer chip is a better fit. The model (584 MB) downloads through Google Play the first time you open Ask, and after that it works offline.

Is it free?

Yes. There are no ads, subscriptions or in-app purchases.

When can I get it?

Retrieve is in closed testing on Google Play and waiting for Google’s approval. The download links on this page will work as soon as it’s public.
